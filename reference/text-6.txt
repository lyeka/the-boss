6
Example of a Round  
of Negotiations
The following example demonstrates how Influence 
cards work and how turbulent the negotiation round 
can be.
Twelve deals have been made, and the 13th is next. One 
dividend is now worth $5M. The space that the marker 
stands on will give the controlling Boss $30M—an 
enticing sum, but this deal requires five Investor Teams: 
Yellow, Blue, and Red, and two from the list of Orange, 
Purple, and Green.
It is David’s turn, and he resolves to make this deal (or 
at least to try). He controls the Blue and Purple Investor 
placards, which are automatically in on the deal. From 
his hand, he plays the Green and Yellow Team cards.
The other players (and their Investor placards) are Larry 
(Red), Bill (Yellow and Green), and Amy (Orange).
David tells Larry, “If you let me use Red, I’ll pay you one 
divided for $5M.”
Larry considers this offer too low, replying: “I’m not 
interested in anything less than $8M. You’ll be getting 
$22M anyway!”
David can’t deny this argument and accepts Larry’s 
request with gritted teeth—but before he can declare 
the deal closed, Bill sends David’s Blue Investor placard 
on vacation with a Travel card. David successfully fends 
off this attack with a STOP card, placing both cards on 
the discard pile. However, Amy also plays a Travel card 
on Blue, and David has no more STOP cards to defend 
himself. With this Investor placard out of play, Amy 
plays a Blue Team card and offers to let David use it in 
the deal in exchange for a participation fee of $9M.
While David negotiates with her, Larry plays a Boss 
card and announces, “I’m the Boss! Forget David; you 
have to deal with me now.”
“I think not!” retorts David, who plays a Boss card of his 
own to remain the Boss. Despite David’s bold assertion, 
Larry has another Boss card in his hand and plays it. No 
further Boss cards are played—the other players having 
none in hand or declining to play them—so Larry really 
is the new Boss and now controls the deal.
“I have Orange and Purple myself,” says Larry as he 
lays those Team cards in front of him. His Red Investor 
placard is automatically in play. Therefore, he still needs 
Yellow and Blue to complete the deal.
Amy speaks up first: “I was offering Blue to David, but 
it’ll be more expensive for you; you’ll have to promise 
$10M to use it.”
David offers the Yellow Team card that he played at the 
beginning of the round for $10M as well, hoping to still 
get a piece of the pie.
“Mine is cheaper!” says Bill, who offers Larry the use of 
his Yellow Investor placard for $8M.
“No, it’s not!” answers David, who plays three 
Recruitment cards on Bill to steal his Yellow Investor 
placard. Bill regrets not having kept any STOP cards 
to protect against this. “As I was saying, Yellow costs 
$10M,” repeats David. He now has Yellow as both a 
Team card and an Investor placard in front of him.
“Usury!” whimpers Larry, but he agrees quickly before 
the deal gets away again and declares, “Going once, 
going twice, the deal is closed!”
The banker pays $30M to Larry, who pays Amy and 
David $10M each and keeps a profit of $10M for 
himself.
Of the Team cards, Larry’s Orange and Purple are 
discarded since they were needed for the deal, as is 
Amy’s Blue. Naturally, David used his newly recruited 
Yellow Investor placard for the deal and not the Team 
card of the same name, so he could take the latter back 
into his hand. He also takes back his Green Team card 
since it was not needed for the deal.
All played Influence cards are placed on the discard 
pile, as well as the Travel card that was covering David’s 
Blue Investor placard.
Larry, the final Boss, places the top Deal placard on the 
space and rolls the die to determine whether the game 
ends. He rolls a 5, so the game continues, with the 
player to the left of Larry taking the next turn.
